> **Conversions used:** GH (as CaCO3) = 2.497·Ca + 4.118·Mg. KH (as CaCO3) = 0.8202·HCO3. Per 1 mg/L KH from a bicarbonate salt: HCO3 = 1.219 mg/L, plus Na = 0.459 mg/L (from NaHCO3) or K = 0.781 mg/L (from KHCO3). Per 1 mg/L GH from a chloride salt: Cl = 0.708 mg/L (from CaCl2 or MgCl2).

## 1. APAX Lab: product line, composition, dosing, water profile, flavour claims, base water

### Takeaway
APAX Lab (official domain **apaxlab.com**, verified via its product, FAQ and blog URLs) sells pre-blended "flavour profile" concentrates. There are three core products, **TONIK [1], JAMM [2] and LYLAC [3]**. The names in the brief, "JAMAICA" and "LYLAC", are not right except for LYLAC; the actual names are TONIK, JAMM, LYLAC. A fourth product, **KONFLUX [K]**, launched in 2025/26, and there are limited editions. Unlike Lotus or Barista Hustle, **every APAX bottle contains both hardness salts and bicarbonate buffer**, so the products are not a hardness part plus a buffer part. They are blended with each other by gram ratio. APAX **does not publish ion-by-ion mg/L, GH or KH**. It only gives TDS-meter "ppm" figures (about 30 ppm per g/L; 1 drop ≈ 0.065 g ≈ 2 ppm in 1 L), so precise ion contributions cannot be derived from public data.

### Inferences
- **Per-dose increments for the website (best available, TDS-meter ppm only):** 1 g of any APAX concentrate in 1 L adds ≈ 30 ppm on APAX's meter. 1 drop (≈ 0.065 g) adds ≈ 2 ppm in 1 L, ≈ 4.3 ppm in 450 mL, or ≈ 10 ppm in a 200 mL cup. The standard 3–4 g/L dose gives ≈ 90–120 ppm. These are conductivity-meter readings. The meter's conversion factor (NaCl or "442") is not stated, so they are **not** mg/L of dissolved salts and cannot be converted to GH/KH.
- If 3–4 g/L gives roughly 90–120 mg/L of salts, the concentrates are about 3% w/w salt (≈ 30 mg salt per g). This is an order-of-magnitude inference, not a specification.
- From the declared ingredients: LYLAC contributes **no calcium** (Mg only), so its GH is entirely Mg. KONFLUX contributes **no magnesium**, so its GH is entirely Ca. TONIK and JAMM both carry Ca and Mg. Every product adds some alkalinity (Na/K bicarbonate) and chloride. Only LYLAC adds sulfate.
- Implied flavour logic, as the label chemistry suggests (my inference; APAX never states it): TONIK (Na-forward, chloride, bicarbonate likely low) leans "acidity"; JAMM (K-forward, likely more bicarbonate) leans "body/sweetness, softens acidity"; LYLAC (Mg + sulfate) leans "florals and clarity". This matches common community lore. Mg is said to extract fruity notes, and bicarbonate dampens acidity. None of this is verified for these products.
- Because APAX publishes only meter-ppm, the website cannot honestly show Ca, Mg, Na, K, Cl, SO4 or HCO3 increments for APAX. At best it can show "+≈30 ppm TDS per g/L". It should label the ion breakdown "not disclosed".

### Gaps
- No APAX ion concentrations, GH or KH per mL or per drop, or salt masses were found anywhere, including retailers and forums. Measuring them would need a GH/KH titration kit or an ICP lab analysis of a diluted sample.
- The APAX FAQ, dilution chart and "which water base" pages were read only through search summaries. I could not confirm whether the "ppm" figures use a TDS-442 or NaCl scale.
- Country of origin is unconfirmed. The brief says "Italy/Europe?"; no source found stated the company's location.
- KONFLUX dosing in g/L for brewing beyond the generic 3–4 g/L, the meaning of "silica in the form of fine-ground quartz" (quartz is barely soluble), and the composition of the Nemo and Slow Pour limited editions were not found.
- No information on discontinued APAX products. The "Core Box Set" naming suggests a 2025/26 repackaging, but this is unconfirmed.

## 2. Comparison products: composition and resulting profiles

### Takeaway
Only **Lotus** gives enough consistent data to compute exact ion contributions per drop. **Third Wave Water** publishes salts plus target TDS and alkalinity, but not Ca/Mg mg/L. **Aquacode** publishes TDS and its Ca:Mg ratio, and one forum user measured its ions. **Perfect Coffee Water** and **COFFEE WATER** publish only salt names. Barista Hustle is a DIY recipe, not a product. Peak Water (BWT) is a filter jug, not a concentrate. "Holy Water", "Pure Coffee Water", "Mineral Water Tablets" and "Elemental" could not be found as coffee-water products.

## 3. Counter-ions (chloride vs sulfate vs citrate) and buffers (Na vs K bicarbonate): what each brand uses and why

### Takeaway
Two camps exist. **Chloride-based** products are APAX TONIK, JAMM and KONFLUX, Lotus, and PCW. **Sulfate/citrate-based** products are TWW, COFFEE WATER, and Barista Hustle's Epsom recipe. APAX LYLAC is the only APAX product with sulfate. Buffer cations vary: Lotus offers both Na and K, APAX mixes both in every bottle with K emphasised in JAMM and LYLAC, TWW uses K for espresso and Na for dark roast, and PCW uses only Na. Brands rarely give an explicit chemical flavour rationale for the counter-ion. Their flavour language is attached to whole products, not to ions.

### Cited Findings
- APAX salts per product: see the table in section 1. APAX frames the choice in terms of "individual ions and their combinations" rather than GH/KH, but gives no per-ion rationale (D) — [APAX FAQ](https://apaxlab.com/pages/faq)
- TWW: the Classic profile has no bicarbonate (alkalinity from calcium citrate). For espresso, TWW adds potassium bicarbonate, stating that high pressure and temperature increase the risk of minerals separating. Dark roast uses sodium bicarbonate at much higher alkalinity (C for the salts, D for the rationale) — [TWW espresso](https://support.thirdwavewater.com/en-US/about-espresso-machine-profile-125766); [TWW EU FAQ](https://thirdwavewater.eu/pages/faq)
- A third-party comparison claims calcium adds body and sweetness and can mute delicate origin flavours, and that the citrate anion adds perceived brightness compared with chloride or sulfate (D, unsourced claim) — [jayarr.coffee](https://jayarr.coffee/blog/coffee-water-recipes-compared/)
- Chloride and machine corrosion: La Marzocco recommends < 30 mg/L chloride, and Synesso recommends 5–15 mg/L depending on pH and alkalinity (C, manufacturer specs as quoted in a forum) — [home-barista PCW thread](https://www.home-barista.com/water/perfect-coffee-water-t74107.html)
- Lotus lets users choose the Na or K buffer independently. Hedrick's "Light & Bright" uses K only, and Rao/Perger uses both (C) — [Scott Rao](https://www.scottrao.com/blog/lotus-water-drops)

### Inferences
- In chloride-based systems, chloride rises in step with GH: about 0.71 mg/L Cl per 1 mg/L GH (as CaCO3). A 90 GH all-chloride water therefore carries about 64 mg/L Cl⁻ (the Lotus Rao/Perger example), which is **above La Marzocco's 30 mg/L guidance**. This is relevant to the APAX "1 g/L max in machine" rule and Lotus espresso recipes.
- K vs Na bicarbonate: per 1 mg/L KH, K adds 0.78 mg/L versus Na 0.46 mg/L. Brands that prefer K usually cite taste (less "salty"), but no source found here gave that rationale for a specific product.

### Gaps
- No brand publishes controlled sensory data separating counter-ion effects (Cl vs SO4 vs citrate) from cation effects.
- Coffee ad Astra and Barista Hustle articles on chloride vs sulfate taste were not retrieved in this session.

## 4. Adding a concentrate to bottled mineral water instead of RO: arithmetic and brand warnings

### Takeaway
The arithmetic is linear: final ion = base ion + Σ(dose × per-unit increment), with alkalinity and GH also additive. One exception: an increment measured as TDS-meter ppm (APAX) is only approximately additive in conductivity terms. Among the brands, **APAX** explicitly addresses non-demineralised bases. It allows soft water under 30 ppm and reduces the dose by the base's ppm. Retailers warn that hard bottled or tap water leaves "less room". TWW, Aquacode and Lotus specify distilled or RO water. Barista Hustle explicitly endorses using its concentrates to boost a bottled water's hardness.

### Cited Findings
- APAX: "designed for distilled or demineralised water". If unavailable, use a soft base (ideally < 30 ppm). Dose = target − base. For a 60 ppm base, add 1–2 g/L to reach 90–120 ppm (C, manufacturer) — [APAX FAQ](https://apaxlab.com/pages/faq); [APAX "Which water base"](https://apaxlab.com/blogs/news/which-water-base-to-use-with-apax-lab)
- Nordic Brew Lab warns that tap, bottled and spring water already contain many minerals, so APAX has less room and overdosing raises hardness (D) — [Nordic Brew Lab](https://nordicbrewlab.com/pages/apax-lab)
- Aquacode: the sachet is to be diluted in "7 litres of pure distilled water (0 ppm)" (C) — [Ozone Coffee](https://ozonecoffee.co.uk/products/aquacode-7l)
- TWW: packets are designed for a gallon of distilled water (C) — [Coffee ad Astra](https://coffeeadastra.com/2018/12/16/water-for-coffee-extraction/)
- Barista Hustle: its concentrates are "helpful if [you] just want to boost the hardness of your favourite bottled water" (C/D) — [Barista Hustle](https://www.baristahustle.com/diy-water-recipes-the-world-in-two-bottles/)
- Example base: Volvic TDS ≈ 109 mg/L, already near or above APAX's whole target range — [Wikipedia Volvic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvic_(mineral_water))

### Inferences
- For the website: use the Lotus per-drop table and the Aquacode/TWW per-sachet increments for ion-level addition to a bottled water's label values. For APAX, show only "+30 ppm TDS per g/L (+2 ppm per drop per L)" and compare it with the bottle's dry-residue (TDS) figure. Warn when base TDS + increment exceeds APAX's 90–120 ppm range, or when base TDS is above 30 ppm (APAX's own threshold).
- Bottled water labels give dry residue at 180 °C (mg/L). This is not the same as a conductivity-meter ppm, so mixing the two is approximate. Bicarbonate-rich bottled waters will push KH far above what any concentrate recipe assumes, and no concentrate can lower it.

### Gaps
- Lotus's and Perfect Coffee Water's own guidance on non-RO bases was not found. The title of Rao's Lotus post mentions "a great hack to find the best water", but its content was not retrievable.
- No brand gives guidance on handling the high bicarbonate of bottled waters, for example acidifying.

## 5. Independent verification of compositions and flavour claims

### Takeaway
Independent evidence is thin. There is **one forum ion measurement for Aquacode** (consistent with its claims), **Coffee ad Astra's alkalinity check of TWW Classic** (value not retrieved), a single blog's GH/KH readings for TWW and Lotus that conflict with TWW's own spec, and user anecdotes about Lotus drop variability. **No independent lab analysis, blind triangle test or structured tasting of APAX products was found.** All APAX flavour claims remain grade D.

### Cited Findings
- Aquacode: a home-barista user measured Ca 6.3, Mg 16.9, Na 0.6, K 0.6, hardness 85 and alkalinity ≈ 30 (C, consistent with the manufacturer's 85 ppm and 1:2.7) — [home-barista](https://www.home-barista.com/water/aquacode-for-espresso-t81766.html)
- Coffee ad Astra (Jonathan Gagné) describes TWW Classic and Espresso as pre-weighed packets for a gallon of distilled water, and added a January 2019 update after measuring Classic's total alkalinity with a photometer. The value was not retrieved — [Coffee ad Astra](https://coffeeadastra.com/2018/12/16/water-for-coffee-extraction/)
- homeespressolab.com reports TWW Espresso at TDS ~120, GH 70, KH 35, and Lotus at TDS ~125, GH ~70, KH ~40. This **conflicts** with TWW's official ≈ 150 TDS and ≈ 80–94 alkalinity for the espresso profile. The method is unknown and the figures may come from older formulas (C/D, low reliability) — [Home Espresso Lab](https://homeespressolab.com/guides/water-for-espresso/)
- Lotus users report dose variability from drop technique (D) — [home-barista](https://www.home-barista.com/water/lotus-water-experiences-t82817-10.html)
- APAX's own suggested self-test: brew one batch with demineralised water, split it into four cups (one control, plus TONIK, JAMM and LYLAC drops), and taste. This is not a blind panel and has no published results (D) — [APAX FAQ](https://apaxlab.com/pages/faq)
- The SCA Best New Product 2026 award for KONFLUX is reported only by retailers. It is a product award, not a sensory validation (D) — [Kaffebox](https://www.kaffebox.no/product/apax-lab-konflux-mineral-concentrate/)

### Inferences
- Most APAX "endorsements" come from stockists (Scott Rao, Nordic Brew Lab, Slow Pour Supply) and are not independent.
- The website should label all flavour descriptors as manufacturer claims (D). For APAX it should show composition as "ingredients declared; quantities undisclosed".

### Gaps
- No James Hoffmann, Lance Hedrick (except as Lotus co-founder), Barista Hustle, Perfect Daily Grind or Reddit structured APAX test was found. Reddit was not reachable through search.
- No ICP or titration data exist for any APAX product, PCW, COFFEE WATER, or the current TWW formulas.
