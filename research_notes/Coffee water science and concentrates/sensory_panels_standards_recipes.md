# Coffee brewing water: sensory-panel evidence, industry standards and recipes (to Oct 2026)

**Research-access caveat (read first).** During this session, every direct page fetch failed with DNS or proxy errors. That included royalcoffee.com, dailycoffeenews.com, baristahustle.com, coffeeadastra.com, home-barista.com, eightouncecoffee.com and archive.org. Nearly every finding below therefore comes from **search-engine snippets and summaries of the cited pages**, not from full-text reads. Exact quotes marked "snippet" come from search-result extracts of the cited URL. Read the primary page before publishing a number verbatim. Anything I could not confirm against even a snippet is in the "Gaps" sections.

**Evidence grades used throughout:**
- (A) peer-reviewed controlled sensory study
- (B) peer-reviewed but indirect (chemistry, computation or extraction only, or sensory work on drinking water)
- (C) structured professional panel (size and blinding recorded where known)
- (D) informal tasting, blog, forum or marketing

**Conversions:** GH (as CaCO3) = 2.497·[Ca] + 4.118·[Mg]. KH (as CaCO3) = 0.8202·[HCO3−]. The reverse factors I used are Ca = GH_Ca/2.497, Mg = GH_Mg/4.118 and HCO3 = KH/0.8202. Counter-ion masses come from stoichiometry:
- Na = 0.3768 × HCO3 (from NaHCO3)
- K = 0.6408 × HCO3 (from KHCO3)
- SO4 = 3.952 × Mg (from MgSO4)
- Cl = 2 × 35.45/40.08 × Ca (from CaCl2) and 2 × 35.45/24.305 × Mg (from MgCl2)

Values I calculated are flagged "calc." and all are in mg/L.

## 1. SCA water standards (2009 SCAA standard → 2016 SCAE chart → 2018 SCA Water Quality Handbook / Coffee Standards): exact targets and their basis

### Takeaway
There are two families of SCA numbers, and they are often confused.
- **The 2009 SCAA "Water for Brewing Specialty Coffee" point targets:** TDS 150, Ca hardness 68 (17–85), alkalinity 40, pH 7.0, Na 10, chlorine 0.
- **The later SCA (2016 SCAE chart, 2018 Handbook/Standards) ranges:** hardness 50–175 ppm CaCO3, alkalinity 40–70 or 40–75 ppm CaCO3, pH 6–8.

I found no published sensory study that underlies either set. The lower alkalinity bound of 40 is explicitly justified by corrosion protection, and the remaining numbers read as expert consensus with equipment-protection input. Evidence grade overall: **(D)/(C) consensus**.

### Cited Findings
- **2009 SCAA standard (revised 21 Nov 2009), as reproduced in a water-treatment company's copy.** For a "superior cup", the table gives calcium hardness as 4 grains or 68 mg/L, with an acceptable range of 1–5 grains (17–85 mg/L), plus a TDS target of 150 mg/L and a pH target of 7.0. SCA's own link to the document is dead. — [Urbansaqua copy of SCAA Standard (PDF)](https://www.urbansaqua.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/www.urbansaqua.com-robust-ro-drinking-water-systems-water-standards.pdf); [beansandbarista version comparison](https://beansandbarista.com/blog/sca-water-standard/)
- **Full 2009 target set as listed by secondary sources:** TDS 150 (acceptable 75–250), Ca hardness 68 (17–85), total alkalinity 40 ("at or near 40"), pH 7.0 (6.5–7.5), sodium 10, chlorine 0, odour clean/fresh, colour clear. — [Coffee ad Astra, Water for Coffee Extraction (2018), snippet](https://coffeeadastra.com/2018/12/16/water-for-coffee-extraction/); [Pact Coffee blog](https://blog.pactcoffee.com/blog/best-water-for-coffee-brewing); [open-exam-prep Q-grader guide](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/brewing-science-water-chemistry/sca-water-standards)
- **Calc, 2009 ion equivalents:** a Ca hardness of 68 that comes entirely from Ca means Ca ≈ 27.2 mg/L, with the 17–85 range giving 6.8–34.0 mg/L Ca. Alkalinity 40 means HCO3 ≈ 48.8 mg/L. Na is 10 mg/L.
- **2016 SCAE chart:** recommended total hardness was 50–175 ppm CaCO3, described as the "core zone" for espresso machines and hot-water boilers. — [beansandbarista](https://beansandbarista.com/blog/sca-water-standard/); [BWT reprint, "Charting Water for Better Coffee", Café Europa winter 2016](https://www.bwt.com/de/-/media/bwt/global-data/documents/literatur-pou-ps/reprint_cafe-eu_winter_2016_22-26.pdf?rev=ee492c2471c94e969acb7ddac327e6d6)
- **2018 SCA Coffee Standards:** these reportedly kept 50–175 but relabelled it "calcium hardness" rather than total hardness, and lowered the alkalinity ceiling from 75 to 70, giving 40–70. The SCA's 2021 home-brewer standard reuses alkalinity 40–70 and pH 6–8. — [beansandbarista comparison table](https://beansandbarista.com/blog/sca-water-standard/)
  - **Calc for 50–175 as Ca only:** Ca ≈ 20–70 mg/L.
  - **Calc for 40–70 KH:** HCO3 ≈ 48.8–85.3 mg/L.
- **Basis of the alkalinity floor:** the SCA Water Quality Handbook "suggests that levels below 40 ppm (as CaCO3) can result in too little buffer to prevent corrosion at boiling temperatures". The upper bound is 75 ppm "when using harder water levels". — [Daily Coffee News, Practical Water Guide Part II (Kornman, 29 Aug 2018), snippet](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2018/08/29/a-practical-water-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-ii-the-sensory-data/); [Royal Coffee republication](https://royalcoffee.com/treading-water-a-practical-h2o-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-ii-the-sensory-data/)
- **Handbook sensory claims, second-hand:** "high carbonate content … carbonate-heavy water produced the most bitter, had the highest pH … and tasted flat", and higher alkalinity lowers perceived acidity. Panel details are not given in any source I could reach. — [beansandbarista summary](https://beansandbarista.com/blog/sca-water-standard/)
- **Two SCA cupping specifications exist and must be distinguished.** For example, SCA-102 cupping specifies Ca hardness 50–175, alkalinity at or near 40–70 and pH 6–8. — [open-exam-prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/brewing-science-water-chemistry/sca-water-standards)
- **Academic critique (B):** Wellinger, Smrke & Yeretzian (2017) cite the SCAA optimum (total hardness 68, range 17–85; alkalinity 40) but argue that "the existence of a single optimum value for total hardness and alkalinity is most probably unlikely", because the best value depends on the coffee. — [Wellinger et al., "Water for Extraction—Composition, Recommendations, and Treatment", in *The Craft and Science of Coffee* (2017), ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/312183310_Water_for_Extraction-Composition_Recommendations_and_Treatment)
- **SCA educational framing (World of Coffee session "Measure, Aim, Treat: How to use the SCA Water Guide"):** higher total hardness tends to bring out sweet and fruity notes, and higher alkalinity reduces acidity and can flatten the cup. (D/consensus) — [World of Coffee event listing](https://worldofcoffee.org/site-calendar/measure-aim-treat-how-to-use-the-sca-water-guide-to-your-advantage)

### Inferences
- The 2009 point targets (68/40/150) are the numbers most cited in recipes, especially as "SCA water" in calculators. The current SCA documents use wide ranges (50–175 hardness) that mainly reflect equipment protection. The website should state which one it means.
- Labelling the 2018 range "calcium hardness" contradicts the 2016 "total hardness" label. This matters because many popular recipes are magnesium-only and would read as Ca hardness = 0.
- Nothing I found indicates that any SCA number came from a published, replicated sensory panel. The best description is "expert consensus with equipment constraints" (grade D/C).

### Gaps
- I could not access the 2015/2018 SCA Water Quality Handbook text. Its exact sodium and chlorine targets, its pH statement and any in-house tasting data (number of tasters, blinding) are unverified. The "most bitter / highest pH / flat" carbonate statement is second-hand.
- The 2009 standard's origin is unconfirmed. Claims that it derives from the Coffee Brewing Center / Lingle-era work did not appear in any search result.
- I could not confirm Hendon's or Wellinger's authorship role in the 2018 Handbook from this session.

## 2. Royal Coffee / The Crown, "Treading Water: A Practical H2O Guide for Coffee Professionals" (Daily Coffee News, Aug 2018)

### Takeaway
The series is by Chris Kornman (Royal Coffee / The Crown, Oakland). It ran in Daily Coffee News as Part I (15 Aug 2018) and Part II (29 Aug 2018) and was republished on royalcoffee.com as "Treading Water". The sensory work in Part II is a small, internal professional panel run over two rounds, so it is grade (C) with small n. Its results:
- **Calcium water** was preferred overall on light-roast washed coffees.
- **Magnesium** was liked for sweet and floral attributes in light roasts, but its mouthfeel and sweetness became "overwhelming" and "cloying" as roasts darkened.
- **The sodium-bicarbonate water** gave a thicker, bubblier crust.
- **Distilled water** earned the highest tallied score in Round 2.

I found no "Part III" on water. A royalcoffee.com page labelled "Part III: Revival" appears to be about Café de Olla.

### Cited Findings
- **Publication facts:**
  - Part I, "Chemistry & Conditioning", ran in Daily Coffee News on 15 Aug 2018. It includes a worked example for making 4 L of water to set hardness/alkalinity targets and explains the ppm-as-CaCO3 convention. — [DCN Part I](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2018/08/15/a-practical-water-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-i/); [Royal Coffee Part I](https://royalcoffee.com/treading-water-a-practical-h2o-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-i-chemistry-conditioning/)
  - Part II, "The Sensory Data", ran on 29 Aug 2018 under Chris Kornman's byline. It describes "two well-documented rounds of taste-testing, using different mechanisms for recording our data", carried out at the Crown. — [DCN Part II](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2018/08/29/a-practical-water-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-ii-the-sensory-data/); [Royal Coffee Part II](https://royalcoffee.com/treading-water-a-practical-h2o-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-ii-the-sensory-data/)
- **Panel:** the snippets describe a "small internal panel of the Crown team". Named cuppers include "Richard and Jen". Exact n is not retrieved. — [DCN Part II, snippet](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2018/08/29/a-practical-water-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-ii-the-sensory-data/)
- **Blinding:** the Home-Barista discussion thread is titled "Royal Coffee Blind Tasting of Waters", which implies the tastings were blind. I did not confirm this from the article itself. — [Home-Barista thread](https://www.home-barista.com/water/royal-coffee-blind-tasting-waters-t54215.html)
- **Calcium.** "The calcium-based formula [was] preferred overall by cuppers in every light-roast washed coffee except one, with magnesium running a close second, mostly with lighter roasts." In Round 2 the calcium water was "more acidic" with "high ratings across the panel, second by just a hair to the distilled water in total point score". One panelist found that the SCA-"ideal" calcium water "looks great by the cupper ratings, [but] felt a little bland in terms of flavor expression". The article also describes calcium as "a good extractor, bonding readily with coffee acids" with a limescale risk. — [DCN Part II, snippets](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2018/08/29/a-practical-water-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-ii-the-sensory-data/); [Royal Coffee Part II, snippet](https://royalcoffee.com/treading-water-a-practical-h2o-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-ii-the-sensory-data/)
- **Magnesium.** Tasters found "lots of sweet and floral attributes, particularly in lighter roasts. However, especially as roasts darkened, many tasters found the mouthfeel overwhelming, and sweetness cloying." The authors call magnesium a good "accent" mineral: "a pinch can bring out some nice sweetness, and when paired with calcium it can help balance a coffee's profile." — [Royal Coffee Part II, snippet](https://royalcoffee.com/treading-water-a-practical-h2o-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-ii-the-sensory-data/)
- **Bicarbonate.** High bicarbonate lowers perceived acidity, impedes extraction and makes the bloom or crema bigger. The sodium-bicarbonate water "produced a noticeably thicker and bubblier crust" in Round 2. Dosing example: "just 0.1 g [baking soda] in 1 liter of distilled water will get you about 50 ppm (alkalinity as CaCO3)". Calc check: 0.1 g/L NaHCO3 gives 72.6 mg/L HCO3, so KH ≈ 59.6. The article's "about 50" is a rounding. — [DCN Part II](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2018/08/29/a-practical-water-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-ii-the-sensory-data/); [Royal Coffee Part II](https://royalcoffee.com/treading-water-a-practical-h2o-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-ii-the-sensory-data/)
- **Alkalinity references quoted in the article:** the SCA floor is 40 and its upper bound is 75 ppm as CaCO3 for harder water. Colonna-Dashwood & Hendon's *Water for Coffee* is cited as suggesting "around 80 ppm bicarbonate HCO3− (about 130 ppm alkalinity as CaCO3)". — [DCN Part II, snippet](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2018/08/29/a-practical-water-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-ii-the-sensory-data/)
  - **Contradiction (calc):** 80 mg/L HCO3 is only about 66 ppm as CaCO3, and 130 ppm as CaCO3 would need about 159 mg/L HCO3. Either the snippet or the article contains a unit slip. Verify before quoting.
- **Distilled water.** In Round 2, distilled water "performed very well, earning the highest tallied point score as well as top ratings from both Richard and Jen". It was described as having "a balanced acidity, decent sweetness, and a silky, smooth mouthfeel". — [DCN Part II, snippet](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2018/08/29/a-practical-water-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-ii-the-sensory-data/); [Royal Coffee Part II](https://royalcoffee.com/treading-water-a-practical-h2o-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-ii-the-sensory-data/)
- **Forum summary of the overall result:** the panel "preferred low carbonate, and slightly preferred calcium to magnesium hardness. Most surprisingly, they liked distilled water a lot!" — [Home-Barista thread](https://www.home-barista.com/water/royal-coffee-blind-tasting-waters-t54215.html)
- **Pushback from the same thread (D):** other posters ran dozens of public blind French-press tastings in which distilled-water brews were "always the outlier", "thin and harsh" or "lacking in sweetness and flat but not awful". — [Home-Barista thread](https://www.home-barista.com/water/royal-coffee-blind-tasting-waters-t54215.html)
- **Follow-up from Royal Coffee:** a later article, "Blending Water: Some Important Math To Help You Mix Waters Of Different TDS", refers back to the two-part 2018 series. — [Royal Coffee](https://royalcoffee.com/blending-water-some-important-math-to-help-you-mix-waters-of-different-tds/)

### Inferences
- The Crown panel is the most-cited professional tasting behind three common beliefs: "Ca for clarity/acidity, Mg for sweetness/florals, keep Mg low for dark roasts", and "bicarbonate dulls". Its weight is limited by a small n, a single lab, a mostly light-roast washed sample and no reported statistics.
- The distilled-water result contradicts the Pangborn (1971) finding that distilled water gives excessively sour coffee. It does match the consumer studies in section 7. This suggests that trained cuppers in a cupping format may not penalise mineral-free water as much as standards assume.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the exact recipes (mg/L of Ca, Mg, HCO3, Na) for each test water, the coffee list and origins, the precise roast levels, the panel n per round, the scoring form, or the confirmation of blinding. Royalcoffee.com and DCN both failed DNS.
- The brief's claim that "bicarbonate-only waters were least sweet" was **not verified** in any snippet. The verified bicarbonate claims are lower acidity, a thicker crust and impeded extraction.
- No "Part III" was found. If the brief's "parts I–III" refers to another instalment, it was not indexed.

## 3. Barista Hustle (recipes, The Water Course) and Colonna-Dashwood & Hendon, *Water for Coffee* (2015)

### Takeaway
**Barista Hustle (BH)** recipes use magnesium only (Epsom salt) for hardness and NaHCO3 for buffer. They are justified by Hendon's 2014 computational chemistry and by small, unblinded in-house cuppings, so grade (D). The one documented BH cupping had two tasters (Matt Perger and Jem), who preferred +40 mg/L Mg²⁺ over +20 mg/L and over the control.

**_Water for Coffee_ (2015)** is self-published. Its flavour claims rest on Hendon et al. 2014 (J. Agric. Food Chem.), a density-functional-theory binding-energy study with **no sensory panel** (grade B, indirect). Later experimental chemistry (Bratthäll et al. 2024) found acid extraction is largely independent of Ca/Mg.

### Cited Findings
- **Hendon, Colonna-Dashwood & Colonna-Dashwood (2014).** "The Role of Dissolved Cations in Coffee Extraction", *J. Agric. Food Chem.* 62(21):4947–4950. The authors used density functional theory to "quantify the thermodynamic binding energies of five coffee acids, caffeine, and … eugenol" with Na⁺, Mg²⁺ and Ca²⁺. Popular summaries say high Mg²⁺ "increase[s] the extraction of coffee into water and improve[s] the taste", and that flavour depends on the balance of ions and bicarbonate. The abstract mentions no sensory test. — [Univ. of Bath research portal](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/the-role-of-dissolved-cations-in-coffee-extraction/); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/262111309_The_Role_of_Dissolved_Cations_in_Coffee_Extraction) **(B)**
- **Phys.org press coverage (June 2014).** "The science behind the perfect coffee" popularised the "magnesium extracts more" claim. — [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2014-06-science-coffee.html) **(D, press)**
- **Experimental counter-evidence: Bratthäll, Figueira & Nording, *Heliyon* 2024.** They added MgCl2 or CaCl2 pre- versus post-brew and measured by GC-MS/NMR. "Extraction of acids proceeds independent of the water composition in most cases", while "interactions taking place post-brew may, however, influence the perceived flavor". They also note that the concentrations at which Ca/Mg become perceivable in coffee (300 ppm Ca²⁺, 200 ppm Mg²⁺) are "typically not reached in tap water". — [PMC10907646](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10907646/) **(B)**
- **Earlier Swedish work:** a 2021 Swedish bachelor thesis cites a study by Fekete in which adding Ca/Mg chlorides pre- or post-brew produced no difference in perceived flavour. — [DiVA thesis PDF](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1546046/FULLTEXT01.pdf) **(D, unpublished student work)**
- **Maxwell Colonna-Dashwood's sensory viewpoint:** an interview/article on European Coffee Trip covers his practitioner view. I could not read its specific claims in this session. — [European Coffee Trip](https://europeancoffeetrip.com/water-for-coffee-maxwell-colonna-dashwood/); [Colonna blog "The water keeps on flowing"](https://colonnacoffee.com/blogs/maxwell/the-water-keeps-on-flowing) **(D)**
- **Hendon on Mg:** a secondary page paraphrases him as calling Mg²⁺ "extremely sticky" relative to Ca²⁺. — [The Basic Barista, Lotus recipes](https://thebasicbarista.com/en-us/blogs/article/lotus-brewing-water-recipes) **(D)**
- **Hendon profile:** "Christopher Hendon Aims to Simplify Coffee with Science", Barista Magazine. — [Barista Magazine](https://www.baristamagazine.com/professor-chris-hendon-simplifies-coffee-with-science/)
- **BH concentrate system:** two concentrates designed so that "1 g of concentrate = 1 ppm GH (Mg solution) or KH (bicarbonate solution) after dilution in 1 L". The original buffer is 8.6 g baking soda and the original hardness concentrate is 25 g Epsom salt, each made up to 500 g deionised water. — [BH, DIY Water Recipes Redux (2019)](https://www.baristahustle.com/diy-water-recipes-redux/); [BH, The World in Two Bottles](https://www.baristahustle.com/diy-water-recipes-the-world-in-two-bottles/) **(D)**
- **"Barista Hustle Water" (#4):** multiple secondary sources give GH 80 / KH 40, with hardness from Epsom salt only. — [jayarr.coffee comparison](https://jayarr.coffee/blog/coffee-water-recipes-compared/); [BH recipe page](https://www.baristahustle.com/barista-hustle-water-recipe/)
  - **Calc ions:** Mg 19.4, HCO3 48.8, Na 18.4, SO4 76.7, Ca 0.
- **BH "Melbourne" recipe:** described as "very 'soft' water, low in mineral content and useful for those long filter brews or cuppings drawn out over 5 to 10 minutes". — [BH TWC 4.06 Basic Brew Water Recipes](https://www.baristahustle.com/lesson/twc-4-06-basic-brew-water-recipes/)
- **BH Water Course cupping (TWC 5.04, "Magnesium Cupping").** The setup was a control (Melbourne brew water) against +20 mg/L Mg²⁺ and +40 mg/L Mg²⁺. "Both [tasters] were clearly in favour of the coffee prepared with 40 milligrams of Mg2+", and they preferred 20 mg over the control. **Panel = 2 (Perger, Jem). No blinding or statistics reported.** — [BH TWC 5.04](https://www.baristahustle.com/lesson/twc-5-04-magnesium-cupping/) **(D)**
  - **Calc:** +20 / +40 mg/L Mg equals +82 / +165 GH as CaCO3 on top of the base water.
- **BH course overview:** TWC 5.03 "Innovation and Research" is a course lesson. — [BH](https://www.baristahustle.com/lesson/twc-5-03-innovation-and-research/)
- **Matt Perger's original Epsom + baking-soda water:** Home-Barista moderator "Pat" calculated it as total hardness 100 mg/L and alkalinity 50 mg/L as CaCO3, Mg only, with sulfate ≈ 91 mg/L. He flagged corrosion risk for espresso boilers. Another page reproduces the recipe. — [Home-Barista thread](https://www.home-barista.com/water/matt-pergers-water-recipe-for-coffee-is-it-ok-safe-for-espresso-machines-what-do-you-think-t42255.html); [Velodrome Coffee, Matt Perger Water Recipe](https://www.velodromecoffeecompany.com/pages/matt-perger-water-recipe) **(D)**
  - **Calc ions:** Mg 24.3, HCO3 61.0, Na 23.0, SO4 ≈ 96. This is close to Pat's 91, and the difference comes from rounding of the inputs.

### Inferences
- The industry's "magnesium boosts extraction and sweetness" narrative is anchored to a computational paper (B) plus small, unblinded demonstrations (D). It does not rest on a controlled sensory study. Experimental work since 2014 (Heliyon 2024, Copenhagen 2026) has not supported large Mg- or Ca-driven extraction or flavour effects at realistic doses.
- BH's own 2-person cupping is consistent with the Crown's "Mg = sweet/florals in light roasts", but it is far too small to separate real effects from expectation, especially since the tasters knew the hypothesis.

### Gaps
- I could not access the *Water for Coffee* (2015) book text. Its specific recommended recipes (target Mg, Ca, HCO3), any espresso versus filter split and its exact flavour wording are unverified. The book is also cited as recommending ~80 ppm HCO3 (see the unit contradiction in section 2).
- No "Pure Water" course by Barista Hustle was found. The BH course is called "The Water Course" (TWC). The brief may be conflating the two.
- I could not confirm the exact Mg and HCO3 targets of each numbered BH recipe (#1–#4 and others).

## 4. Community and professional recipes: ion compositions, GH/KH and flavour claims

### Takeaway
Most community recipes sit in a narrow band: **GH ≈ 50–100 and KH ≈ 20–50 ppm as CaCO3**, with Mg-heavy hardness and low bicarbonate. They are lower in alkalinity than the SCA's 40–70 or 40–75 band for filter. Espresso recipes from Lotus run lower GH (~20) and higher KH (45–55).

Every flavour claim attached to these recipes is grade (D) except the generic Crown (C) panel observations. Published GH/KH for the "same" recipe differ between sources, notably Rao/Perger.

**Notes from the sources behind the table:**
- **Lotus drop arithmetic (Rao):** with 1 L as the reference volume, each Ca or Mg drop adds 20 ppm GH and each Na or K drop adds 10 ppm KH. With 450 mL as the reference volume, 1 drop adds about 10 ppm hardness or 5 ppm alkalinity.
  - **Rao/Perger in drops per litre:** 7 Ca, 13 Mg, 8 Na, 11 K.
  - **Contradiction:** at 20 ppm per drop, 7 + 13 = 20 drops would give 400 ppm GH, not 90. The per-drop figure must refer to a different basis, so the snippet's quantities need checking against the page.
  - Rao advises raising Mg and HCO3 rather than Ca when testing higher-hardness waters, because "calcium can create scale but magnesium cannot". — [Scott Rao](https://www.scottrao.com/blog/lotus-water-drops)
- **Chloride caution:** chloride-based recipes push Cl⁻ above many espresso-machine makers' limits, even at a GH of 20–50. A Home-Barista commenter adds that claims about Ca and Mg taste effects "are not at all established". — [Home-Barista Lotus thread](https://www.home-barista.com/water/lotus-water-experiences-t82817.html) **(D)**
- **Common descriptor mapping in community writing:** "calcium tends to accent sweetness … but maintain clarity, while magnesium helps add complexity of flavor and mouthfeel" (Home-Barista commenter). "Too much hardness … bitterness and astringency; too little … weak and tea-like" (Basic Barista). — [Home-Barista Lotus thread](https://www.home-barista.com/water/lotus-water-experiences-t82817.html); [The Basic Barista](https://thebasicbarista.com/en-us/blogs/article/lotus-brewing-water-recipes) **(D)**
- **WBrC context:** European Coffee Trip has a piece titled "Secrets of Making Water for World Brewers Cup Championship". Its contents were not retrieved. — [European Coffee Trip](https://europeancoffeetrip.com/secrets-of-making-water-for-world-brewers-cup-championship/)

### Inferences
- **Filter versus espresso split in community practice:**
  - Filter recipes cluster at GH 60–90 / KH 20–50, Mg-forward.
  - Espresso recipes from Lotus cluster at GH ~20 / KH 45–55, which protects boilers and limits scale.
  - The SCA's 50–175 hardness range is broad enough to contain all of the filter recipes on total-hardness terms, but not on "calcium hardness" terms, because most of these recipes are Mg-only.
- **Roast level in community practice:** "Holy Water" and Rao/Perger are recommended for light roasts. No controlled data supports a specific recipe for a specific roast. The direction (lower KH for light roasts to keep acidity) is consistent with the peer-reviewed alkalinity findings in section 6.

### Gaps
- Exact current Barista Hustle numbered-recipe ion values, Coffee ad Astra's own table, the Third Wave Water Classic and Espresso compositions, and James Hoffmann's or Lance Hedrick's recipes were not retrievable.
- The champions' waters other than Medina and Jaafar (e.g. 2016–2025 WBrC champions) were not found with verifiable GH/KH. Sprudge has a 2025 champion recipe article that I did not read: [Sprudge 2025](https://sprudge.com/how-to-brew-coffee-like-the-2025-world-brewers-cup-champion-338576.html).

## 5. Other structured or informal tastings: Five Senses, NZSCA, Kaffeemacher, Nucleus, BWT/ZHAW, Hoffmann, Hedrick, Coffee ad Astra

### Takeaway
Outside the Crown and the peer-reviewed work, the tastings are mostly grade (D).
- **Five Senses (AU)** found no clear extraction effect of MgCl2 up to 500 ppm.
- **NZSCA regional blind tastings** split, with one city preferring the lowest alkalinity and hardness and two preferring local tap water.
- **Kaffeemacher's 18-water test** favoured moderate GH/KH.
- **The ZHAW (Wellinger/Yeretzian) panel**, 3 trained cuppers, blind, in triplicate, found a 1:3 Mg:Ca ratio more bitter and astringent at fixed hardness 70. This is the most structured non-journal tasting (C).

I found no verified water tasting by James Hoffmann or Lance Hedrick.

### Cited Findings
- **ZHAW / Yeretzian group (C, reported in a trade reprint and a book chapter).**
  - Design: filter coffee at a fixed total hardness of 70 ppm CaCO3 with the Mg/Ca ratio varied. The panel was "three trained cuppers in a blind triplicate design".
  - TDS of the brews did not change across ratios.
  - Astringency and bitterness were "significantly higher" at a 1:3 Mg/Ca ratio, i.e. calcium-rich. BWT, a company selling Mg-dosing filters, presents this as "bitterness and astringency are decreased significantly with more magnesium".
  - — [BWT reprint, Café Europa 2016 (PDF)](https://www.bwt.com/en/-/media/bwt/global-data/documents/literatur-pou-ps/reprint_cafe-eu_winter_2016_22-26.pdf?rev=14c2136ff20a4a1abfeb0f8c0718708a); [BWT Magnesium Technology (marketing)](https://www.bwt-wam.com/en/technologies/bwt-magnesium-technology/); [Wellinger et al. 2017 chapter](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/312183310_Water_for_Extraction-Composition_Recommendations_and_Treatment)
  - **Conflict of interest:** BWT is a commercial beneficiary.
- **Five Senses Coffee (Jeremy Hartley, Australia) (D).**
  - Design: Clever Dripper brews with RO water spiked with MgCl2 at six levels from 0 to 500 ppm, with no Ca and no alkalinity, measured with a refractometer.
  - Result: "a trend, but it doesn't appear to be statistically significant, even when comparing pure RO against 100 ppm". Conclusion: "the way you pour liquid into your CCD is affecting the brew much more than the amount of magnesium".
  - This was an extraction (TDS) test, not primarily a sensory one.
  - — [Five Senses blog](https://fivesenses.com.au/blogs/news/experimenting-with-the-effect-of-water-quality-on-coffee); [Home-Barista thread](https://www.home-barista.com/water/royal-coffee-blind-tasting-waters-t54215-10.html)
- **Separate refractometer test in the same forum thread:** a cupping group "were unable to confirm the postulation of higher TDS using high GH water". — [Home-Barista](https://www.home-barista.com/water/royal-coffee-blind-tasting-waters-t54215-20.html) **(D)**
- **NZSCA Roaster Guild, "There's something in the water" (2015).** Blind tastings ran in three cities. "Auckland tasters seemed to follow the science, preferring water with the lowest alkalinity and hardness", whereas Wellington and Christchurch favoured their local water. The panel sizes were not retrieved. — [NZSCA 2015](https://nzsca.org/2015/05/26/roaster-guild-theres-something-in-the-water-results/) **(C/D)**
- **Kaffeemacher (CH/DE).**
  - Design: one blogger tasted 18 water samples and measured GH, alkalinity, conductivity and pH.
  - "Low alkalinity made the coffee taste harsh and sharp, while very high total hardness and alkalinity made it flat." The best results were around 8 °dH GH and 5 °dH alkalinity (≈143 / 89 ppm as CaCO3, calc).
  - This is notably harder and more alkaline than the community recipes.
  - — [Kaffeemacher](https://kaffeemacher.de/en/blogs/kaffeewissen/water-for-coffee-results-and-recommendations) **(D)**
- **Polish/Czech educational cuppings (European Coffee Trip).** Single-mineral waters were tasted. Notes: Mg "seemed to heighten acidity, sweetness and body", Ca gave "rounder sweetness and creaminess", and the most favoured sample was TDS 300 ppm with Ca:Mg 1:1. — [European Coffee Trip, Olomouc water cupping](https://europeancoffeetrip.com/coffee-water-educational-cupping-olomouc/) **(D)**
- **Nucleus Torréfacteurs (Canada).** In their own blind tasting of a Costa Rican coffee, both Third Wave Water profiles (Classic and Espresso) beat tap water on acidity, and the Classic was the favourite for cupping. — [Nucleus Coffee](https://nucleuscoffee.com/en/blogs/specialty-coffee/how-to-make-brewing-water) **(D)**
- **Third Wave Water review (Podium Coffee Club):** the difference was clear against hard tap water but "marginal against already-filtered water". — [Podium Coffee Club](https://podiumcoffeeclub.com/blogs/blog/third-wave-water) **(D)**
- **Chris Shotwell blog (2023):** personal filter experiments starting from Holy Water. — [chrisshotwell.com](https://www.chrisshotwell.com/2023/01/11/exploring-water-in-filter-coffee/) **(D)**
- **Coffee ad Astra (Jonathan Gagné):**
  - "Water for Coffee Extraction" (Dec 2018) reproduces the SCA targets and the Rao 2013 recipe and discusses alkalinity/buffer.
  - "A Tool and Videos for Crafting Custom Brew Water" (Aug 2019) provides a recipe calculator.
  - A Patreon post covers preparing the Rao/Perger concentrate.
  - These are chemistry explainers, not sensory panels.
  - — [Coffee ad Astra 2018](https://coffeeadastra.com/2018/12/16/water-for-coffee-extraction/); [Coffee ad Astra 2019](https://coffeeadastra.com/2019/08/23/a-tool-and-videos-for-crafting-custom-brew-water/); [Patreon](https://www.patreon.com/posts/video-on-rao-27493575) **(D)**

### Inferences
- The ZHAW result (Ca-rich → more bitter and astringent) partially agrees with the Crown "Mg = sweeter" impression. It conflicts with the Crown finding that calcium water was the overall favourite. Both are small panels, and they used different coffees and formats.

## 6. Peer-reviewed controlled sensory studies linking water composition to descriptors

### Takeaway
The controlled literature is thin. Its most robust finding is that **alkalinity/bicarbonate, not hardness, drives the sensory changes**.
- **Copenhagen 2026 (A):** hardness, even with Ca above drinking-water levels, produced no marked flavour change. Bicarbonate reduced acidity and complexity.
- **Pangborn & Trabue 1971 (A, older method):** NaHCO3 water gave the most bitter coffee, CaCl2 the sourest, NaCl slightly salty, and distilled water "excessively sour".
- **Imamura 2024 (A/B):** sodium reduces "clean taste".
- **Bratthäll 2024 (B):** acid extraction is largely independent of Ca/Mg.

The Mg-versus-Ca "sweetness versus clarity" lore has no controlled-study support.

### Cited Findings
- **University of Copenhagen, "Impact of Water Composition on Coffee Sensory Properties: A Comprehensive Analysis", *Journal of Food Science* 91(9), e71434 (2026).**
  - Design: ultrapure water compared with added Ca, Mg and bicarbonate, from the "ideal brewing zone" to extremes. Coffee: a single Colombian ("Kachalu"). Methods: duo-trio screening plus descriptive sensory analysis.
  - Findings: "water hardness alone produced no marked flavor changes, even when calcium was added at concentrations above those typically found in drinking water". "Alkalinity, not hardness, was the primary determinant of sensory changes". Bicarbonate levels "matter for acidity and complexity". Small differences in Ca+HCO3 samples were attributed to CaCO3 precipitation reducing effective bicarbonate, not to calcium's own taste.
  - — [KU research portal](https://researchprofiles.ku.dk/en/publications/impact-of-water-composition-on-coffee-sensory-properties-a-compre/) **(A)**
  - Panel size and exact concentrations were not retrieved.
- **Pangborn & Trabue (1971), *J. Food Sci.* 36:355ff.**
  - Design: coffee, tea and flavoured drinks made with distilled water plus 750 ppm of single salts.
  - Findings: coffee was "most bitter" with NaHCO3, "sourest" with CaCl2 and slightly salty with NaCl. In review summaries, "carbonates were the worst offenders, resulting in a bitter, flat coffee", and coffee from distilled water was "excessively sour".
  - **Pangborn 1982**, with natural mineral waters: visual differences in the brew were often larger than flavour differences.
  - — Summarised in [Wellinger et al. 2017](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/312183310_Water_for_Extraction-Composition_Recommendations_and_Treatment) and ["The impacts of water compositions on sensory properties of foods and beverages cannot be underestimated", *Food Res. Int.* 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S096399691830200X) **(A, dated; 750 ppm is far above brewing-water levels; full text not read)**
- **Imamura (Miyashita Ice Co.), "The clean taste of coffee depends on the sodium content of its extraction water", *Food Sci. Technol. Res.* 30(2), 2024.**
  - Melted-ice water had lower Na and gave coffee that sensory evaluation rated as having an "improved clean taste".
  - Adding Na (as an organic-acid salt) increased taste-sensor saltiness and decreased "clean taste".
  - Author affiliation is with an ice company, which is a potential conflict of interest.
  - — [J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/fstr/30/2/30_FSTR-D-23-00129/_article); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/377709196_The_clean_taste_of_coffee_depends_on_the_sodium_content_of_its_extraction_water) **(A/B; panel details not retrieved)**
- **Navarini & Rivetti (2010), "Water quality for Espresso coffee", *Food Chemistry*.** Espresso-focused review/experiments linking bicarbonate to crema/foam. Not read in full. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0308814609004725) **(B)**
- **Bratthäll, Figueira & Nording, *Heliyon* 2024:** Ca/Mg do not materially change acid extraction, though post-brew interactions may affect perception. — [PMC10907646](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10907646/) **(B)**
- **Korean CSHR 2017, "Analysis of Correlations between Mineral Contents in Waters and Sensory Characteristics of Coffee".**
  - Design: five mineral waters plus a distilled control.
  - Mineral content changed flavour, acidity, bitterness and body. The hedonic test showed "an inverse linear relationship between taste quality and total mineral contents".
  - — [KoreaScience](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO201721242143947.page) **(A, small regional journal; panel details not retrieved)**
- **Indonesian pour-over study (IOP Conf. Ser. Earth Environ. Sci. vol 102, 012095), "The influence of brewing water characteristic on sensory perception of pour-over local coffee".** It exists, but its findings were not retrieved. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322828281_The_influence_of_brewing_water_characteristic_on_sensory_perception_of_pour-over_local_coffee); [IOP PDF](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1755-1315/102/1/012095/pdf)
- **Drinking-water context (not coffee):**
  - Japanese study 2023: "good-tasting" water had more Ca²⁺, K⁺ and SiO2 and less Mg²⁺/SO4²⁻ (rough) and Cl⁻ (bitter). — [search summary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10907646) **(B; attribution in snippet uncertain)**
  - Hong et al. 2026, *J. Sensory Studies*: purified and mildly mineralised drinking water were liked most, and excess minerals lowered acceptance. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/joss.70121?af=R) **(B)**

**Descriptor-by-descriptor synthesis (grade of best supporting evidence in brackets):**
- **Acidity (brightness):**
  - Decreased by bicarbonate/alkalinity [A, Copenhagen 2026; C, Crown; D, SCA].
  - Calcium water was "more acidic" [C, Crown].
  - CaCl2 gave the "sourest" coffee at 750 ppm [A, Pangborn 1971].
  - Distilled water was "excessively sour" [A-dated, Pangborn] versus "balanced acidity" [C, Crown]. **These contradict each other.**
- **Florals and fruit:** linked to Mg in light roasts [C, Crown]. "Higher hardness → sweet and fruity" [D, SCA WoC session]. No controlled support.
- **Sweetness:**
  - Mg gave "sweet", but "cloying" in dark roasts [C, Crown].
  - Ca gave "rounder sweetness" [D, ECT].
  - Purified/low-TDS water was most liked by consumers [A, Kang 2022].
  - No controlled study isolates sweetness versus a specific ion.
- **Body/mouthfeel:**
  - Mg was "overwhelming" in dark roasts [C, Crown].
  - Distilled was "silky, smooth" [C, Crown].
  - NaHCO3 gave a thicker crust/crema [C, Crown; B, Navarini].
  - "Too little hardness → weak, tea-like" [D].
- **Bitterness and astringency:**
  - Most bitter with NaHCO3 [A-dated, Pangborn].
  - Higher with a Ca-rich (1:3 Mg:Ca) ratio [C, ZHAW].
  - "Too much hardness → bitterness/astringency" [D].
- **Flatness/dullness:** high alkalinity/carbonate [A, Copenhagen (reduced complexity); A-dated, Pangborn; D, SCA, Kaffeemacher].
- **Harsh/sharp:** very low alkalinity [D, Kaffeemacher]. Contrast this with the Crown distilled-water result.
- **Clarity/"clean":** low sodium [A/B, Imamura 2024]. Ca "maintains clarity" [D].
- **Chocolate/roast notes:** **no study found** that links any ion to chocolate or roast descriptors. Only the 2025 JFCA hardness × roast finding (section 7) touches dark-roast taste.

### Inferences
- For a science-based website, the defensible statements are:
  - (1) alkalinity/bicarbonate is the dominant flavour lever (it mutes acidity and flattens the cup);
  - (2) hardness ions at realistic levels have small or undetectable effects in controlled tests;
  - (3) sodium and chloride can add saltiness or reduce "cleanness" at higher levels.
- The "Mg for sweetness/florals, Ca for body/clarity" heuristics should be labelled grade C/D.

### Gaps
- No controlled study reports panel-validated descriptors for florals, fruit, chocolate or "clarity" as functions of Ca:Mg at brewing-relevant levels.
- I could not retrieve the panel size of the Copenhagen 2026 study or the concentration grid.
- I did not read the full text of Pangborn 1971.

## 7. Do water preferences differ by roast level or processing? And consumer versus expert preferences

### Takeaway
There are two peer-reviewed signals for roast interaction. Neither is a definitive controlled expert-panel trial.
- **Kang, Piao & Ko 2022 (A, consumers, n = 167):** liking fell as TDS rose (0 → 100 → 200 ppm) at medium and dark roasts.
- **2025 *J. Food Compos. Anal.* filter-brewing study (A/B):** high hardness favoured dark roasts and low hardness favoured light roasts.

**These contradict each other on dark roasts.** Kang finds purified water best for dark, while the 2025 study finds hard water better for dark. Crown's practitioner panel (C) found Mg's heavy mouthfeel off-putting in dark roasts.

**Processing:** I found no study that tests water × processing (washed versus natural).

**Consumer versus expert:** consumer and student panels repeatedly prefer distilled, purified or low-mineral water (Korean 2020, n = 103; Korean 2017; Kang 2022, n = 167). Experts assume some hardness is needed. The Crown panel's high score for distilled water is a partial expert counter-example.

### Cited Findings
- **Kang, Piao & Ko (2022), "Effects of water types and roasting points on consumer liking and emotional responses toward coffee", *Food Quality and Preference* 101:104631.**
  - Design: purified, tap and Australian bottled water, with TDS ≈ 0, 100 and 200 ppm, across three roast points. That gives nine samples rated by 167 consumers.
  - "All sensory attributes had significant differences, except for aftertaste."
  - "Consumer liking decreased in relation to increasing TDS at the medium and dark roasting points." "Coffee brewed using purified water was preferred by coffee consumers at medium and dark roasting points."
  - The snippet's statement about medium/dark leaves the light-roast result unclear.
  - — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0950329322001069) **(A, consumer)**
- **"Exploring the impacts and mechanisms of water on the taste extractions and perceptions of coffee brews: a case study in filter brewing", *J. Food Composition and Analysis* (2025).** "Water samples of high/low hardness values benefited the extraction of taste compounds and the exhibition of secondary dominant tastes for coffees of dark/light roasting degrees, respectively." The proposed mechanisms (hardness affecting compound–water contact and detachment from coffee proteins) are hypotheses. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0889157525008026) **(A/B; panel size and hardness values not retrieved)**
- **Crown panel (2018):** Mg's sweet and floral attributes were liked in lighter roasts, but "especially as roasts darkened, many tasters found the mouthfeel overwhelming, and sweetness cloying". The Ca preference was established on light-roast washed coffees. — [Royal Coffee Part II](https://royalcoffee.com/treading-water-a-practical-h2o-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-ii-the-sensory-data/); [DCN Part II](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2018/08/29/a-practical-water-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-ii-the-sensory-data/) **(C)**
- **Korean J. Food Sci. Technol. 52(5):435–440 (2020).**
  - Design: coffee brewed with distilled water versus medium- and high-hardness water. Panel: 103 university students (56 female, 47 male).
  - The distilled-water ("L") coffee was most preferred (≥ 42%). Bitterness, acidity and body reportedly did not differ significantly.
  - The authors note that student preferences may differ from those of coffee experts, who may favour a certain hardness.
  - — [KoreaScience PDF](https://www.koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO202031458603203.pdf) **(A, consumer/student)**
- **Korean CSHR 2017:** hedonic liking showed an inverse linear relationship with total mineral content. — [KoreaScience](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO201721242143947.page) **(A)**
- **Expert counterpoints:**
  - The Crown's professional cuppers gave distilled water the top tallied score in Round 2 [C].
  - Home-Barista posters' public blind tastings found distilled "thin and harsh" [D].
  - Pangborn 1971 found distilled "excessively sour" [A-dated].
  - — [DCN Part II](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2018/08/29/a-practical-water-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-ii-the-sensory-data/); [Home-Barista](https://www.home-barista.com/water/royal-coffee-blind-tasting-waters-t54215.html); [Food Res. Int. 2018 review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S096399691830200X)
- **Processing × roast (no water factor):** a 2024 study of honey, natural and full-wash processing across light, medium and dark roasts found medium roast best for liking. It did not vary water. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/397854981_Physicochemical_and_Sensory_Qualities_of_Arabica_Coffee_Coffea_arabica_obtained_from_Different_Processing_Methods_and_Roasting_Degrees) **(not water-related; included to document absence)**
- **Brewed coffee with RO/carbon-filtered water:** roast-level liking varied by consumer cluster. One cluster (n = 10) disliked darker roasts, and the others (n = 84) showed no consistent trend. — [Foods 2022, 11(2):180](https://www.mdpi.com/2304-8158/11/2/180) **(A; water not varied)**

### Inferences
- **Light roasts:** low alkalinity and low hardness. This is supported by the 2025 JFCA study, practitioner recipes and the Crown's light-roast findings.
- **Dark roasts:** the evidence splits. The 2025 study says harder water helps extraction, the consumer study (Kang 2022) says purified is liked best, and the Crown says avoid Mg-heavy water. A website should present this as unresolved.
- **Consumer versus expert:** non-expert panels (students and consumers) consistently prefer the least-mineralised water. Experts' insistence on some hardness rests on extraction theory and grade C/D cupping. The gap may partly reflect format, since cupping slurries tasted over 5–10 minutes differ from drinking a cup, and familiarity with tap-water coffee.

### Gaps
- **Water × processing:** no study testing water composition against processing (washed versus natural) was found in any grade.
- **Expert versus consumer, same samples:** no study directly compares trained Q-graders and consumers on the same water series.
- **Unretrieved details:** the 2025 JFCA study's hardness levels and panel size, and the light-roast result in Kang 2022.

## 8. Contradictions register (summary)

### Takeaway
The main conflicts are over distilled water, Ca versus Mg, dark-roast hardness, Rao/Perger's numbers, SCA labels and units, and the size of the Mg extraction effect.

### Cited Findings
- **Distilled water:**
  - Top score at the Crown [C], and most liked by consumers [A] — [DCN Part II](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2018/08/29/a-practical-water-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-ii-the-sensory-data/); [Kang 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0950329322001069); [Korean 2020](https://www.koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO202031458603203.pdf)
  - versus "excessively sour" [A-dated] — [Food Res. Int. review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S096399691830200X)
  - and "thin and harsh" [D] — [Home-Barista](https://www.home-barista.com/water/royal-coffee-blind-tasting-waters-t54215.html)
- **Calcium versus magnesium:**
  - Ca preferred overall [C, Crown] — [DCN Part II](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2018/08/29/a-practical-water-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-ii-the-sensory-data/)
  - versus a Ca-rich ratio being more bitter and astringent [C, ZHAW] — [BWT reprint](https://www.bwt.com/en/-/media/bwt/global-data/documents/literatur-pou-ps/reprint_cafe-eu_winter_2016_22-26.pdf?rev=14c2136ff20a4a1abfeb0f8c0718708a)
  - versus hardness having no marked effect [A] — [Copenhagen 2026](https://researchprofiles.ku.dk/en/publications/impact-of-water-composition-on-coffee-sensory-properties-a-compre/)
- **Dark roast:**
  - Harder water benefits extraction [A/B] — [JFCA 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0889157525008026)
  - versus purified water most liked [A] — [Kang 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0950329322001069)
  - versus Mg "overwhelming" [C] — [Royal Coffee Part II](https://royalcoffee.com/treading-water-a-practical-h2o-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-ii-the-sensory-data/)
- **Mg extraction effect:**
  - Increases extraction [B, DFT] — [Hendon 2014](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/the-role-of-dissolved-cations-in-coffee-extraction/)
  - versus no significant TDS change up to 500 ppm MgCl2 [D] — [Five Senses](https://fivesenses.com.au/blogs/news/experimenting-with-the-effect-of-water-quality-on-coffee)
  - versus acid extraction independent of Ca/Mg [B] — [Heliyon 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10907646/)
- **Rao/Perger GH/KH** is reported variously as 90/42, ~89/40, 69/40, 76/50 and 75/25. — [Scott Rao](https://www.scottrao.com/blog/lotus-water-drops); [jayarr](https://jayarr.coffee/blog/coffee-water-recipes-compared/)
- **SCA hardness label:** "total hardness" (2016) versus "calcium hardness" (2018). Alkalinity ceiling: 75 versus 70. — [beansandbarista](https://beansandbarista.com/blog/sca-water-standard/)
- ***Water for Coffee* bicarbonate:** "80 ppm HCO3 ≈ 130 ppm as CaCO3" is arithmetically inconsistent (calc: 80 HCO3 ≈ 66 as CaCO3). — [DCN Part II snippet](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2018/08/29/a-practical-water-guide-for-coffee-professionals-part-ii-the-sensory-data/)
- **Kaffeemacher's best** (~143 GH / 89 KH) is much harder than community light-roast recipes (60–90 GH / 20–50 KH). — [Kaffeemacher](https://kaffeemacher.de/en/blogs/kaffeewissen/water-for-coffee-results-and-recommendations)

### Inferences
- These conflicts are best explained by differences in format (cupping versus drinking, filter versus espresso), coffee (single-origin light washed versus dark), panel type (consumer versus trained) and small sample sizes. They do not undermine the alkalinity finding, which is consistent across grades.
